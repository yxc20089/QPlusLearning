The observation contains the maze, remaining normal/power pellets, named ghost positions/headings/modes, current power mode, phase, legal moves, lives, a 12-position history and visits to the current tile. It **does not contain** destination visit counts, time since the last pellet, exact remaining power/phase timers, queued ghost turns/reversals or actor pixel/speed-cycle offsets. A 12-step history cannot contain the preceding 24-step circuit. The teacher accesses fuller native state than the learner; two visibly similar positions can require different actions because of those private details. Fix that information gap rather than asking the model to infer invisible state. Present pellet/ghost facts are already visible, so missing fields alone do not explain every wrong choice.

There is also a substantial collection gap: **all 4,096 training states have three lives**, while **234/251 supplied decisions happen after a death**. Death resets release counters and phase timing while global elapsed frames continue. The old evaluation has only **23/256 survival-critical search decisions, 8/256 with danger within one maze tile, three heavily revisited positions, and no post-respawn or ≤30-pellet endgame examples**. Averaging agreement across that set can improve while live play remains poor. This trace consumes one power pellet and eats zero ghosts; it cannot establish power-expiry or ghost-hunting skill.

The teacher also needs repair. Its five-seed CPU report raises mean score to 3,590 but produces **zero clears and four capped runs**. At turn 135 its 20-move search reports both actions eventually dying and selects the immediately fatal one; at turn 195 three actions have exactly equal search values. Lifetime visit penalties can discourage necessary transit through empty corridors. We should not multiply these labels and assume more examples will fix them.

While auditing every counterfactual, we found a further native **rewind omission**: `ghostReleaser.save/load` does not preserve the global/personal release-counter mode. A hypothetical move can switch it after a respawn and contaminate restored gameplay. The new CPU benchmark augments that rewind hook to preserve both counter sets and mode; forward physics, v1 action semantics and learner observations are unchanged. It then matches all 251 trace states exactly. The v2 teacher must inherit this repair before collecting multi-life examples.

The [ordered v2 plan](evaluation/pacman-v2-plan.json) has seven gates:

1. **Freeze and measure v1.** Run both verified adapters under the new v1 gameplay protocol and preserve per-seed traces. The completed checkpoints and current dataset remain available.
2. **Fix and version the action contract.** Test native centre crossings, reversals, tunnels, speed phases, pellet pauses, ghost-eating pauses, deaths and respawns. Never reinterpret v1 replay records silently.
3. **Align teacher and learner state.** Expose current timers/movement phase and compact progress/coverage facts. Keep replay seeds, RNG state, future teacher paths and labels out of model input. Test token budgets and identical training/live formatting.
4. **Validate the teacher in complete games.** Rank immediate safety first, then survival and pellet progress per simulation time. Add maze-cleanup routes and a penalty for no-progress cycles, allowing necessary revisits. Handle genuine ties and all-search-die cases explicitly. Require actual level clears and audited safety/progress improvement before bulk generation.
5. **Collect the states Kev actually visits.** Use validated teacher corrections on learner errors, then aggregate in rounds, following the distribution-shift motivation of [DAgger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686). Include ghost danger, junction branches, loops, remaining-pellet cleanup, power acquisition/expiry/hunting and respawns. Keep complete games and related forks in one split; permanently reserve benchmark seeds. Seed 7 becomes a training/regression case if its failures are used for learning.
6. **Pilot the faithful Kev recipe.** Keep rank-16 all-target LoRA, the 256-dimensional head, Skills warm start and decision-v7 replay. Review up to 2,048 pilot boards before growing to 8,192–16,384. ## Gameplay benchmark

[gameplay_benchmark.py](gameplay_benchmark.py) and [benchmark-spec.json](benchmark-spec.json) replace the notebook's single 128-decision, first-death illustration with five seed-paired games, three lives, a **512-decision / 180-active-simulation-second cap**, and native game-over/level-clear termination. Five seeds are reserved away from every packaged dataset split. A cap is recorded as unresolved, never as a victory. This is still the same classic maze and does not imply held-out-layout generalization.

The main measurements are pellets collected, level clears, **avoidable immediate deaths**, no-pellet cycles and stalls, and first-life/post-respawn progress. Secondary diagnostics count power pellets, ghosts eaten, fruit, heading bias, skipped immediately safe pellet opportunities and stationary/multi-tile actions. An avoidable immediate death means another legal move from the identical native state/RNG avoids capture until the next decision boundary; it is not a claim about long-term optimality. The benchmark distinguishes all-immediate-actions-fatal states and empty safety denominators. Counterfactuals only score decisions: they never choose Kev's action or insert a hidden safety controller.

The notebook writes **`comparison.json` schema 2**, with `gameplay.general`, `gameplay.fine_tuned` and per-seed `gameplay.paired` differences. The `before`/`after` snapshot results retain teacher agreement and add stratum denominators; absent strata have `accuracy: null`. Full benchmark requests, responses, adapter identity and diagnostics go to `results/gameplay-general/` and `results/gameplay-fine-tuned/` and are included in the submission export. At most 5,120 gameplay calls plus 512 secondary snapshot calls are required; measure serving latency rather than promising a duration.
