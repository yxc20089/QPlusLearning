Let's open up the teacher using one real decision. This is seed 91009 at level one, after 172 actions in the public qualification replay. The small maze on the left shows the actual tiles around Pac-Man. P marks Pac-Man. BP marks Blinky and Pinky, who occupy the same tile, and In marks Inky. Clyde is elsewhere in the full native state. The crop is just for this drawing.

Pac-Man is moving down. Blinky and Pinky approach from above, while Inky approaches from the right. There is a pellet immediately to the right. It looks attractive if we only ask where the nearest food is. The teacher instead tests every legal first direction in the native engine, including that dangerous direction. The table shows what each direction's best continuation predicts.

Start with Right. It eats that pellet, but loses a life six native frames into the same tile transition. Now look at Up. Up survives its first transition. However, the recent positions already end with this sequence: the current tile, the tile above, then the current tile again. Moving Up closes the two-tile dry cycle. Its continuation loses a life at frame nine. Left survives both sampled futures and eventually reaches a pellet at frame 137. It consumes thirteen pellets before the forecast ends at frame 484.

These are native forecasts. The engine moves the ghosts during Pac-Man's action. It keeps their different target rules, release and chase/scatter clocks, pixel offsets, movement phases, eating pauses and power behavior. At this moment, there is no power time left. A dangerous ghost means an outside ghost that is not frightened. Frightened ghosts do not form the danger barrier in the routing heuristic. The teacher samples two independent future random-generator seeds, rather than looking up the live episode's future randomness. The two forecasts happen to agree here.

How does the teacher build each continuation? It first caches breadth-first-search distances across the walkable maze, including the tunnels. These are shortest maze distances. It then runs a food-routing policy under a buffer of one, two, four or six tiles. At each later simulated decision, it tests every legal next-tile move for immediate survival. If at least one move survives, the continuation chooses among those surviving moves. If every move is fatal, it still has to choose a legal move. This filter operates inside the teacher's simulation. It never replaces Kev's live choice. All legal first moves stay available to Kev.

The inner routing policy also has ordered priorities. First it penalizes a next tile one maze step or less from a dangerous ghost. Then it penalizes separation smaller than the chosen buffer. Next comes completing a dry cycle, unless that next tile contains food. Only then does it compare the food cost shown at the bottom. So that scalar cost is one component of the inner heuristic, not the teacher's whole objective.

Let's read that cost. J with subscript b is the food-route cost for a particular buffer b. The letter v is the candidate next tile. P is the collection of remaining pellets, including power pellets. The letter p is one possible food target in P. The function d measures the cached BFS maze distance between two tiles. G is the collection of dangerous ghosts, and g is one ghost in that collection. We take the distance from v to p, then penalize targets that lie within buffer b of a dangerous ghost. The positive-part notation means keep the value if it is positive, otherwise use zero. The sum adds that penalty across dangerous ghosts. The minimum chooses the cheapest food target. Finally, N of v counts visits to that tile in the recent dry history, limited to the last 64 positions. The two-times-N term discourages repeating that empty route. Both factors of two are heuristic constants in this frozen implementation. A penalty near a target does not guarantee a safe route to it. After this cost, the inner rank prefers greater separation capped at b plus two, then keeping the current heading.

The outer rank rho compares what actually happened in those simulated continuations. Read its entries from left to right. E is the worst immediate-death flag across the futures. C is the worst flag for closing the real root dry cycle. O penalizes an imminent forecast cycle, using the positive part of 32 minus its first occurrence in native frames. D counts the futures that lose a life. If any future dies, S is the minimum number of simulated frames across the futures. Its negative value prefers more survival time. Otherwise this entry is zero. T is the worst time to the next actual pellet. A future with no pellet uses the horizon plus 600 as its fallback value.

The second row continues the same ordered vector. K is the largest forecast cycle count. J is the root food-route cost we just discussed. W counts futures that clear the level, so negative W prefers more clears. F bar is the mean number of pellets consumed, and its negative value prefers more pellets. U is the longest dry spell in forecast decisions. M bar is the mean number of simulated frames. The first unequal entry decides the comparison. These values never become a weighted reward sum, and raw game score is not an entry in this rank.

Right loses at E. Up loses at C. The teacher therefore selects Left before either tempting food value can reverse the result. For Left, all four buffers tie in the first five entries. Its next-pellet time is 304, 273, 169 and 137 frames as the buffer increases from one to six. Buffer six wins at T. Interestingly, its root food cost J is three, while buffer one's is two. That shows why a static food cost alone cannot explain the teacher's final choice.

After choosing Left, we execute only that first direction. Then the teacher reads the real next state and replans. The nominal horizon here is a 480-frame budget because danger is nearby. Each action completes its native boundary, which explains why the surviving forecast ends at 484. This limited portfolio gives a useful decision, with complete-game qualification and separate checks on training continuations still required. It does not prove a globally optimal route or survival in every future.
