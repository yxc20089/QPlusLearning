Before we ask Kev to avoid a ghost, we should know what it can see. Our request contains structured game state. We give it the native maze, the current actors, the clocks and a short record of earlier movement. The model has to learn how those facts should change its choice.

Start with the maze on the left. This drawing uses a small piece of the actual native tile map. A dot is a normal pellet worth ten points. The larger o is a power pellet worth fifty. A space is an empty corridor. When Pac-Man consumes either kind of food, that tile becomes a space. So an uneaten power pellet tells the model where it can activate frightened mode, while the frightened flags and remaining timer tell it whether that mode is active now. We do not need to leave an o behind to show that Pac-Man ate one.

Now look at the actors. We pass Pac-Man’s row and column, but also its pixel offset within the tile. That matters because the game moves in pixels, and our decision boundary is entry to an adjacent tile. We also provide its heading, movement phase, eating pause and queued directions. A row and column alone would hide some of that timing.

We keep Blinky, Pinky, Inky and Clyde separate. Their names tell the model which native personality is present. In chase mode, Blinky targets Pac-Man directly. Pinky aims ahead of Pac-Man. Inky’s target depends on Pac-Man and Blinky together. Clyde switches between chasing and its corner according to distance. The original upward-targeting quirks are preserved. Each ghost also has a current movement mode and frightened flag, so a ghost returning to the house is different from a dangerous ghost outside it.

The pixel offsets, movement phases, next headings and pending reversals or releases are supplied for each ghost too. These are current facts from the engine. They are not a list of future ghost positions. At a new game, some ghosts are still pacing in the house. Native release counters and timers bring them out; we do not replace them with one generic random ghost.

The power timer is especially important. timing.power.remaining_frames tells us how much native frightened time remains. A move may take several simulation frames, and that timer can run out before the move finishes. Seeing a blue ghost at the beginning of a move is therefore not enough to conclude that approaching it will stay safe. Native pauses can hold that clock still, and a ghost flag can change on a different update. We therefore keep the timer and per-ghost state together. The observation also includes chase/scatter time, release state, Elroy state, fruit time and eating pauses.

The third group gives the model a way to recognize its own loops. recent_positions retains up to sixty-four recent positions, with headings. destination_visits tells us how often each legal next tile has been visited, and visits_to_current_tile describes the current tile. decisions_since_last_pellet counts how many decisions have passed without eating another pellet. These are observations of past movement. The model still has to learn when to reverse to survive and when repeated movement is wasting progress.

The equations name the two steps we have just described. g-sub-t is the complete native game state at decision t. O is the observation function that selects the fields we send, and s-sub-t is its result. The game has additional internal state, including its private random generator, that we do not put into the request. a-sub-t is the direction Kev chooses. A-sub-t is the supplied set of wall-legal directions. The membership symbol means its action comes from that set. A legal corridor can still contain a dangerous ghost; legality does not solve the avoidance problem for us.

After the choice, the simulator advances complete native frames until Pac-Man enters the requested adjacent tile or a terminal event occurs. We then form the next observation. The teacher’s future rollouts and preferred label stay outside this input. Next, we will examine how that teacher uses the real simulator to evaluate possible continuations before it creates a training label.
