# QPlusLearning versus Opper Jevman

Read-only source comparison, 2026-10-08. Opper is pinned at [`1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836`](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/tree/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836); our unchanged engine/controller files were inspected at `d9dd85c80bea63d808963bb06226a0ec3f59529e`. This review used browser/GitHub reads and local source inspection. **No model-inference API calls or new gameplay experiments were run.**

Both systems expose the full maze. The largest useful difference is that Jevman computes route facts for each action, whereas our student derives most spatial relationships from the board. Its published scores measure a different game/controller and cannot rank our native-v2/v3 adapters.

## Observation and control

| Dimension | QPlusLearning | Opper Jevman |

Jevman's `TRAP` warning compares ghost tile-distance with Pac-Man tile-distance to the next junction. It is a heuristic, not a collision proof: native speeds, direction restrictions and update timing matter. Its `FRUIT` cue explicitly recommends a route after heuristic safety/reachability filtering; that is more policy assistance than an unranked distance fact. Both derive from current state, but neither should be transplanted as a guaranteed native outcome. [Heuristic definitions, `features.ts:45–46,192–239`](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/blob/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836/src/features.ts#L192-L239)

## Different game dynamics

We execute the verified masonicGIT arcade recreation at `7407174c1d6a38be8cd230577489e39e0873145b`. Its documented residual arcade differences remain in [`source.json`](../vendor/arcade-pacman/source.json); this is not a ROM emulator. Jevman executes its own TypeScript simulator and renderer. The familiar four ghost personalities do not make its trajectories equivalent to ours.

Jevman uses constant speeds by mood, releases Pinky/Inky/Clyde after 1/4/7 seconds, uses a fixed scatter/chase schedule, and routes scripted ghosts by BFS distance to their targets. Frightened ghosts greedily maximize separation. Its power duration is `max(2, 7-level)` seconds, continuously decremented; it has three lives and no bonus-life award. Our native engine retains level-dependent speed/phase tables, pellet-counter release rules, native target-tile steering/reversals, frightened randomness and ghost-eating pauses. The latter means our remaining power clock cannot simply be reduced by elapsed movement frames. [Jevman constants and timers, `sim.ts:85–115,341–368`](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/blob/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836/src/sim.ts#L85-L115), [movement/collisions, `sim.ts:371–510`](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/blob/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836/src/sim.ts#L371-L510), [scripted routing, `features.ts:48–81,220–239`](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/blob/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836/src/features.ts#L48-L81). Our corresponding upstream modules are hash-listed in `source.json` and retained in its verified source archive.

## Teacher and training

Our teacher is native rollout MPC with shortest-food routing: every first legal move, four safety buffers and two independently resampled future-RNG scenarios; 240-frame normal and 480-frame danger/endgame horizons. It ranks immediate safety, imminent cycles, deaths, time to the next pellet and progress, then replans. It never reads the actual episode's future RNG. Full native games on separate qualification seeds and cold replay checks authorize its labels; this is finite-suite evidence, not universal optimality. See [`arcade-teacher.js:52–159`](../games/arcade-teacher.js), [`qualification specification`](../teacher-validation-spec.json) and [`qualification receipt`](teacher-qualification.json).

Jevman's deterministic `greedyChoice()` supplies fallback/scripted moves, not a qualified search teacher. The inspected repository contains no Pac-Man dataset, LoRA training recipe, teacher qualification or distillation pipeline. It evaluates hosted models; `opper/kev-4b` identifies a provider model, not our native-v3 adapter or a weight hash. Our rank-16 all-target LoRA/pointer-head curriculum and task training therefore address an additional problem. [Greedy policy](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/blob/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836/src/features.ts#L220-L239), [model identities](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/blob/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836/shared/models.ts#L7-L18)

## Benchmark and published results

Our repeated reference benchmark uses 20 fixed seed/starting-level pairs across levels 1, 2, 3 and 5. It continues intermediate life losses and stops at first native level clear or game-over. Watchdog exits stay separately censored failures. Same-state/RNG alternate legal actions identify avoidable immediate deaths; the student never receives those outcomes. Traces retain complete requests/responses and adapter identity, plus loops, dry spells, respawn progress, food skips and action geometry. These reference games have informed diagnosis and are not a blind final test. See [`benchmark specification`](../benchmark-spec.json) and [`gameplay_benchmark.py:91–181,225–327`](../gameplay_benchmark.py).

Jevman's committed run uses 100 games per model, repeated level-one starts, continued play across clears, real-time requests and a 300-second playing-time cap, ranked by mean score. It has no seed/level partition in the runner. Its published method says all games exhausted their three lives, with the longest lasting 2 min 24 s; that run was not ended by the cap. Its death labels describe recent corridor/junction/waiting context, rather than counterfactual avoidability, and it has no no-pellet-loop metric/gate. [Runner, `bench.ts:145–205`](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/blob/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836/scripts/bench.ts#L145-L205), [death context, `stats.ts:43–76`](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/blob/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836/src/stats.ts#L43-L76), [published method](https://github.com/opper-ai/jevman-benchmark/blob/1c8b77ac61bafa10956c601b1c9b339dcd277836/index.html)

## What to adopt later

The v4 hard-case generation and exact balanced export are complete and frozen. **Preserve its observation, adjacent-tile actions and teacher.** Adding features now would change the experiment's input contract.

1. Test a separately versioned later observation ablation: add current-state food/power distances, corridor pellet counts and ghost approach/distance facts for each legal adjacent move. Keep the full layered board, native clocks and history. Exclude teacher choices, simulated future outcomes and private RNG; distinguish descriptive geometry from rule recommendations such as `FRUIT`.
2. Measure that ablation on matched complete native games, retaining clears, deaths, avoidability, loops, dry spells and frames per pellet. Add per-seed uncertainty and latency percentiles. Treat deadline/fallback deployment tests as a separate real-time protocol.
3. Consider junction-level actions only as another later experiment: they reduce calls but change available retreat decisions, labels and action semantics, requiring a new protocol and teacher qualification.
4. Adapt the compact replay viewer and request/game version guard. Compare hosted Jev with our adapter only through the same native engine, encoder, action boundaries, seeds and timing rules.

The plausible gap is explicit spatial representation, despite full-board visibility. This comparison does not establish that route features improve our agent or that LoRA rank 16 limits it; a controlled ablation can test the former without disturbing the frozen v4 experiment.
