How does confidence change the score gradient?

The teacher target stays Left. These probabilities are illustrative. Increasing Left’s probability reduces the magnitude of its negative score gradient. This control computes a one-example derivative. It does not run a model or apply an optimizer update.


The remaining probability follows the earlier Up:Right ratio of 0.35:0.45. The score gradients sum to zero. Parameter gradients also depend on the pointer-head features and the backbone’s chain rule, so these three values alone do not determine how much a LoRA weight moves.

What the actual training step does

Kev differentiates the source-record-weighted request loss, accumulates across two microbatches of four requests, clips the global trainable gradient norm to 1.0, then runs fused AdamW on the LoRA matrices and pointer head. OneCycleLR advances after the optimizer step. The balanced-v4 recipe configures a maximum learning rate of 2e−5 and AdamW weight decay of 0.01. The displayed derivative is for one Pac-Man choice; the real update also includes the other requests in its accumulation group and generic replay.

The original pretrained matrices and token embeddings stay fixed. The lab uses rank-16 all-targets LoRA, α = 32, five percent LoRA dropout during training and gradient checkpointing. The backbone remains in the autograd graph. Checkpointing recomputes activations to reduce their storage. BF16 and fused kernels change the execution, while saved LoRA/head weights use FP32.

A new v4 stage starts with completed v3 adapter/head weights and fresh AdamW moments. Resuming v4 recovery restores that stage’s weights, optimizer, schedule and step. Neither the teacher’s search procedure nor the rendered game is differentiated. Teacher labels are fixed training targets. Complete-game performance needs a separate evaluation.
